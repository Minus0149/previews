01The test

What the MOT looks at

An MOT checks that your car meets the legal road-safety and environmental standards. Tap a point on the car, or an item on the list, to see what's inspected.

Top view01 · Lights & signals

Diagram for illustration

  • Headlamps, indicators, brake and fog lights: condition, that they work, the right colour, and headlamp aim.

  • Condition, operation and performance of the brakes, including the parking brake.

  • Condition, security, size and type, and tread depth. The legal minimum for a car is 1.6 mm across the central three-quarters of the tread.

  • Condition and operation of the steering and the suspension components.

  • All required seatbelts present, secure and working, and the driver's seat adjusts and stays put.

  • The exhaust is secure and complete without serious leaks, and exhaust emissions are within the limits for the car.

  • Damage in the driver's view of the road, and that the wipers and washers give a clear view.

  • No excessive corrosion or sharp edges, mirrors present and secure, registration plates and VIN legible, and a working horn.

Reading your result

Pass

Meets the legal standard.

Advisory

Still a pass. Something to watch or fix soon.

Minor

Still a pass. Repair as soon as possible.

Major

Fail. Repair it straight away.

Dangerous

Fail. Don't drive until it's repaired.

Good to know: the MOT doesn't cover the condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox.

02MOT due-date reminder

When is your MOT due?

Pop in the date your current MOT runs out. The gauge shows how long you've got, and the earliest day you can test without losing your renewal date.

  • 01Every year from age three. In Great Britain a car needs its first MOT by its third birthday, then one every year.
  • 02Test early, keep your date. You can have the MOT up to a month (minus a day) before it runs out and keep the same renewal date.
  • 03Lapsed? Without a valid MOT you can only drive the car to a pre-booked MOT test, and it still needs to be roadworthy.
  • 04Not sure of your date? It's on your last MOT certificate, or check it free on GOV.UK with your reg.
