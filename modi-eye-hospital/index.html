Your visit, step by step

No surprises. Here's exactly what happens.

Hospital visits feel easier when you know the order of things. This is the usual path for a first consultation; your doctor may add or skip tests depending on your eyes.

If your eyes are dilated, close-up vision can stay blurry and bright light uncomfortable for a few hours. Bring sunglasses, and ideally someone to drive you home.

  1. 1
    Before you come

    Book, or simply call

    Send a request with the form above or call 080 2349 2233. The team confirms your time and tells you which department you'll see.

  2. 2
    Arrival

    Register at the front desk

    Bring along anything that helps the doctor see the full picture:

    • Current glasses / lenses
    • Old prescriptions & reports
    • List of medicines
    • Sugar / BP history
  3. 3
    Preliminary checks

    Vision and eye-pressure tests

    Reading the chart, checking your glasses power and measuring eye pressure. Quick, painless, and the baseline for everything that follows.

  4. 4
    Consultation

    Time with the specialist

    A detailed examination of the front and back of the eye, with scans or imaging if needed. You'll hear what's going on in plain words.

  5. 5
    After

    A clear plan, and a follow-up date

    Glasses, medicines, a procedure, or simply a review visit. You leave knowing the next step and who to call with questions.

Our story

A name that has meant sight for generations.

The hospital was founded by Padmabhushan Dr. M.C. Modi, a pioneer of free eye-operation camps and the travelling eye hospital in India, who took eye care to people who could not reach it.

What began as a camp-based hospital grew into a full-time super speciality centre, keeping the same idea at its core: expert eye care, made simple and within reach.

  • Community eye care and free eye camps
  • The Leelavathi Modi Eye Bank, supporting corneal transplants
  • Dedicated specialist clinics alongside general eye care

What should I bring to my first visit?
Your current glasses or contact lenses, any old prescriptions and eye reports, a list of medicines you take, and details of diabetes or blood pressure if you have them.
Will my eyes be dilated? Can I drive afterwards?
Many detailed eye examinations use dilating drops, which can leave your vision blurry and light-sensitive for a few hours. It's safest not to drive yourself; bring sunglasses and, if you can, a companion.
Do you see children?
Yes. Paediatric ophthalmology is one of the hospital's specialities, covering squint, lazy eye and other eye problems in children.
